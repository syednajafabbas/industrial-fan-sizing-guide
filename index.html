4 Rules Before Ordering Any Industrial Fan

  • Never buy on free-air CFM alone: Always ask for airflow at your system's actual static pressure (Pa or in. wg).
  • Match Saudi 60Hz power: Confirm 220–240V 1-phase or 380–400V 3-phase at 60Hz on the fan curve and motor nameplate.
  • Pick impeller geometry by resistance: Axial fans handle high volume at low pressure; centrifugal blowers overcome ducting and grease filters.
  • Include a realistic margin: Account for duct leakage, dirty filters, and future balancing dampers (typically 10–15%).

What Is CFM and How Do You Size It Properly?

Understanding volumetric flow rate, standard vs. actual airflow, and exact metric conversions used in Saudi MEP schedules.

CFM Definition & Wikipedia Reference

CFM (Cubic Feet per Minute) is a non-metric unit of volumetric flow rate representing the volume of air (in cubic feet) moving past a cross-section every minute. For the underlying definition and unit background, refer to the Cubic foot per minute (CFM) reference on Wikipedia.

In industrial ventilation, engineers also distinguish between ACFM (Actual Cubic Feet per Minute) at real site temperature and pressure versus SCFM (Standard Cubic Feet per Minute) normalized to standard air density (1.2 kg/m³ at 20°C and sea level). In hot Saudi ambient environments (45–50°C on rooftops), air density drops—affecting mass flow and motor cooling—so always review manufacturer temperature limits.

CFM = (Room Volume in ft³ × ACH) ÷ 60
m³/h = Room Volume in m³ × ACH
1 CFM = 1.699 m³/h = 0.47195 L/s

Static Pressure & The Fan Duty Point

Why two fans with the same "maximum CFM" perform completely differently once connected to ductwork, louvers, or grease filters.

Fan Curve vs. System Resistance Curve

Where Static Pressure Loss Comes From

Static pressure is the resistance a fan must push or pull against, measured in Pascals (Pa) or inches of water gauge (in. wg) where 1 in. wg ≈ 249 Pa. As ductwork gets longer or narrower, system resistance climbs with the square of airflow.

  • Wall shutters & backdraft dampers: typically add 20–50 Pa.
  • Straight galvanized ducting: ~1–3 Pa per meter at typical velocities (6–10 m/s).
  • 90° elbows, transitions & weather hoods: 25–75 Pa per fitting depending on radius.
  • Baffle grease filters & kitchen hoods: 125–300+ Pa (increasing as grease builds up).
  • Bag / HEPA / pre-filters: 100–400+ Pa between clean and dirty conditions.

For ducted runs above 150 Pa, choose backward-curved centrifugal units like the RC-A Centrifugal Roof Fan, CHA Centrifugal Box Inline Fan, or DE / CBA Centrifugal Blowers.

60Hz Electrical Supply & Drive Selection in Saudi Arabia

Avoiding motor overloads and curve mismatches when specifying industrial fans on KSA commercial and industrial grids.

1. Frequency (60Hz vs. 50Hz)

Saudi Arabia uses 60Hz utility power. Because induction motor speed scales with frequency, a 4-pole motor runs near ~1,750 RPM at 60Hz versus ~1,450 RPM at 50Hz. By the fan affinity laws, a 20% speed jump increases airflow by 20%, static pressure by 44%, and brake horsepower by ~73%. Never install a 50Hz-only fan directly on 60Hz without factory 60Hz curve verification.

2. Voltage & Phase in KSA

Standard Saudi commercial/industrial supplies are 220–240V Single-Phase (1Ph, 60Hz) for smaller ceiling, inline, and light wall fans (such as KDK and TAT), and 380–400V Three-Phase (3Ph, 60Hz) for larger roof exhaust fans, industrial blowers, and high-static units.

3. Direct-Drive vs. Belt-Drive

Direct-drive fans have fewer wear parts (no belts or pulleys to tension in high rooftop heat) and pair cleanly with VFDs/speed controllers where rated. Belt-drive units allow pulley ratio changes to fine-tune CFM during Testing & Commissioning (T&C) and keep the motor out of hot or greasy airstreams.

Frequently Asked Questions on Fan Sizing

Why is free-air CFM misleading when selecting an exhaust fan?

Free-air CFM is measured at zero static pressure (0 Pa / 0 in. wg) with no ducts, grilles, dampers, or filters attached. Once installed in a ducted system or behind louvers and grease filters, system resistance reduces actual delivered airflow. Always verify CFM at your project's required static pressure.

How do you convert CFM to m³/h and L/s?

Multiply CFM by 1.699 to get cubic meters per hour (m³/h), or multiply CFM by 0.47195 to get liters per second (L/s). Conversely, divide m³/h by 1.699 to obtain CFM.

Why does 60Hz electrical frequency in Saudi Arabia matter for fan performance?

Saudi Arabia operates on a 60Hz power grid (typically 220–240V single-phase or 380–400V three-phase). A fan curve published at 50Hz runs at a different synchronous motor speed than at 60Hz. Running an unrated 50Hz fan at 60Hz increases RPM by ~20%, which raises power draw sharply by the cube law and can overload the motor.

Should I use an upblast or downblast roof fan for a commercial kitchen?

Always use a grease-rated upblast centrifugal roof fan (such as the RCU Upblast Roof Fan). Upblast units discharge hot, grease-laden air vertically away from the roof membrane and include drainage/curb features, whereas downblast units direct exhaust downward onto the roof.

What information should contractors send for a fast fan quotation in Saudi Arabia?

Provide required airflow (CFM or m³/h), estimated static pressure (Pa or in. wg), mounting type (roof, wall, inline duct, or floor), duct or opening dimensions, application (general air, kitchen grease, warehouse heat, or smoke), voltage/phase (220V 1Ph or 380V 3Ph 60Hz), and delivery city.
